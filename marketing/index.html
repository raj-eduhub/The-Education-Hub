Standard form keeps very large and very small numbers workable: a×10na \times 10^{n} with 1≤a<101 \le a < 10. A measurement is never exact, so a value given to the nearest unit carries an error interval of half a unit either side: 4.6 cm to the nearest 0.1 cm lies between 4.55 cm and 4.65 cm. When rounded values are combined, the bounds combine too, and you must think about which combination gives the largest and smallest result. For a difference or a division the answer may surprise you: the largest value of a−ba - b uses the upper bound of aa with the lower bound of bb.

  • Standard form: a×10na \times 10^{n} with 1≤a<101 \le a < 10.
  • Rounding to the nearest unit gives an error interval of half a unit each way.
  • Upper bounds are written with <<, because the top value is never reached.
  • For a−ba - b, the maximum uses aa's upper bound and bb's lower bound.

Clear explanation

An atom has a tiny nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons in shells. The atomic number is the number of protons and identifies the element; the mass number counts protons plus neutrons. Electrons fill shells from the inside out, holding two in the first shell and eight in the next two, and the number in the outer shell determines how the element reacts. Reactivity trends follow from this: group 1 metals become more reactive down the group as the outer electron is further from the nucleus and more easily lost, while group 7 non-metals become less reactive down the group as an electron is harder to attract.

  • Atomic number is the proton count and identifies the element.
  • Electron shells fill 2, 8, 8 for the first twenty elements.
  • Outer-shell electrons determine chemical behaviour.
  • Group 1 gets more reactive down the group; group 7 gets less.

Key formulas

Worked example: Describe displacement reactions

Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu

Question: What happens when a strip of zinc metal is placed in a solution of copper(II) sulfate? Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction and describe what you would observe.

  1. Use the reactivity series to check whether zinc is more reactive than copper; zinc is above copper, so it can displace copper from copper(II) sulfate.
  2. Write the oxidation and reduction half-reactions: Zn -> Zn2+ + 2e- and Cu2+ + 2e- -> Cu.
  3. Combine the half-reactions to give the overall equation: Zn + Cu2+ -> Zn2+ + Cu; replace Cu2+ with CuSO4 to give the full molecular equation: Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu.
  4. Check that the equation is balanced: Zinc 1 on both sides; Copper 1; Sulphur 1; Oxygen 4. Balanced.
  5. Observations: A reddish-brown coating of copper metal appears on the zinc surface; the solution's blue colour fades as Cu2+ is removed, and the mixture may warm slightly.
  6. Conclusion: This is a displacement reaction; a more reactive metal (zinc) displaces a less reactive metal (copper) from its compound.

Answer: Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu; a solid coating of copper is deposited and the blue colour of the solution fades as Cu2+ is replaced by Zn2+.

Shakespeare wrote scripts for performance, so the meaning lives in what is spoken and done on stage. Dramatic methods include soliloquy, where a character speaks their thoughts aloud to the audience; dramatic irony, where the audience knows what a character does not; and asides, stage directions and shifts between verse and prose. Clear explanation

When Edward the Confessor died childless in January 1066 there were three serious claimants: Harold Godwinson, crowned immediately; Harald Hardrada of Norway; and William of Normandy. Harold defeated Hardrada at Stamford Bridge in September, then marched south and was defeated and killed by William at Hastings in October. William secured his conquest not in one battle but over years, through castle building, the Harrying of the North and the redistribution of land to Norman lords recorded in the Domesday Book. Chronology matters here: the order of events in 1066 explains the outcome.

  • Three claimants, two battles, one year: sequence explains the result.
  • Conquest was secured afterwards, by castles and landholding, not only at Hastings.
  • Domesday Book recorded who held what, and so who owed what.
  • Sources from 1066 were written by people with an interest in the outcome.

Worked example: Use chronology accurately

Question: Put these Norman Conquest events in chronological order from earliest to latest: Edward the Confessor dies; Harold Godwinson is crowned king of England; William sails to England from Normandy; The Battle of Hastings takes place; William is crowned king of England.

  1. Edward the Confessor dies before Harold Godwinson is crowned.
  2. Harold Godwinson is crowned before William sails to England from Normandy.
  3. William sails to England from Normandy before The Battle of Hastings takes place.
  4. The Battle of Hastings takes place before William is crowned king of England.

Answer: Edward the Confessor dies, Harold Godwinson is crowned king of England, William sails to England from Normandy, The Battle of Hastings takes place, and William is crowned king of England.

Clear explanation

Rivers and coasts are shaped by the same four processes: erosion wearing material away, transport carrying it, deposition dropping it when energy falls, and weathering breaking rock in place. In a river's upper course, vertical erosion dominates and produces steep V-shaped valleys, waterfalls and gorges; in the lower course, lateral erosion and deposition produce meanders, ox-bow lakes and floodplains. On coasts, destructive waves erode headlands into caves, arches, stacks and stumps, while constructive waves build beaches and spits. Management is either hard engineering, which resists the process, or soft engineering, which works with it, and each has costs.

  • Erosion, transport, deposition and weathering shape both rivers and coasts.
  • Upper course erodes vertically; lower course deposits.
  • Cave, arch, stack, stump is one sequence with one cause.
  • Hard engineering resists the process; soft engineering works with it.

Worked example: Sequence physical processes

A = width × depth Q = v × A

Question: A river has a width of 6 m, a depth of 1.5 m and a water velocity of 0.8 m/s. Calculate the discharge Q (in m^3/s). Then explain the sequence of physical processes that would occur as the river's energy decreases downstream, leading to deposition.

  1. A = width × depth = 6 m × 1.5 m = 9.0 m^2
  2. Q = v × A = 0.8 m/s × 9.0 m^2 = 7.2 m^3/s
  3. The discharge is 7.2 m^3/s. This is the volume of water passing a cross-section every second.
  4. When the river has high energy, it erodes the bed and banks (hydraulic action and abrasion) and carries sediment.
  5. Sediment is transported in different ways: traction (rolling along the bed), saltation (bouncing along the bed), and suspension (carried in the water column).
  6. Downstream, the river loses energy due to friction and a gentler gradient, so its capacity to carry sediment decreases.
  7. Deposition occurs as velocity falls; heavier particles settle first to form sand or gravel bars, then finer material settles on inside bends and floodplains.
  8. Overall sequence: erosion → transport → deposition as energy decreases downstream.

Answer: 7.2 m^3/s; The sequence of physical processes is erosion, then transport, then deposition as energy decreases downstream.

Clear explanation

Computers store everything as binary, using only 0 and 1, because a circuit is either on or off. Each binary digit is a bit, eight bits make a byte, and the place values in an 8-bit number are 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1, so converting to denary means adding the place values where there is a 1. Text is stored by giving each character a number, as in ASCII, where capital A is 65. An image is a grid of pixels, each storing a colour value, so the file size depends on the number of pixels and the number of bits used per pixel.

  • 8 bits make a byte; place values are 128 down to 1.
  • Characters are stored as numbers, as in ASCII.
  • An image is a grid of pixels, each with a colour value.
  • More pixels or more bits per pixel means a larger file.

Key formulas

Image file size (bits) == width ×\times height ×\times colour depth

Worked example: Convert small binary values

Decimal value = b3×2^3 + b2×2^2 + b1×2^1 + b0×2^0

Question: Convert the small binary value 1011 to its decimal value.

  1. The binary value is 1011, so b3=1, b2=0, b1=1, b0=1.
  2. Apply the conversion formula: Decimal value = b3×2^3 + b2×2^2 + b1×2^1 + b0×2^0.
  3. Calculate each term: b3×2^3 = 1×8 = 8; b2×2^2 = 0×4 = 0; b1×2^1 = 1×2 = 2; b0×2^0 = 1×1 = 1.
  4. Sum = 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11.
  5. Therefore, the decimal value is 11.

Answer: 11

Clear explanation

Mechanisms convert one kind of motion into another. The four types of motion are linear in a straight line, reciprocating backwards and forwards, oscillating swinging about a point, and rotary turning. Levers change the size and direction of a force about a pivot, and the class of lever depends on where the effort, load and pivot sit. Gears and pulleys change speed and torque together: a larger driven gear turns more slowly with more turning force, and the ratio comes from the tooth counts.

  • Linear, reciprocating, oscillating and rotary describe all motion.
  • Levers trade force against distance about a pivot.
  • A larger driven gear means slower rotation and more torque.
  • Gear ratio comes from the tooth counts.

Key formulas

Gear ratio =teeth on driven gearteeth on driver gear= \frac{\text{teeth on driven gear}}{\text{teeth on driver gear}} Mechanical advantage =loadeffort= \frac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}}

Worked example: Calculate mechanical advantage

MA = Load force / Effort force

Question: A lever is used to lift a 60 N load with an effort of 12 N. What is the mechanical advantage of the lever?

  1. Identify the given values: Load = 60 N, Effort = 12 N.
  2. Use MA = Load force / Effort force to calculate: MA = 60 N / 12 N.
  3. Simplify: 60 ÷ 12 = 5.
  4. State the result with units: MA = 5 (dimensionless).

Answer: 5 (dimensionless)
